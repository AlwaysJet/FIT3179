International Visitors to Australia

Exploring international tourism patterns and visitor arrivals

Top 10 Source Countries for Visitor Arrivals

New Zealand recorded the highest number of international visitor arrivals in July 2026, followed by China and the United States. The remaining source countries recorded lower arrival numbers, showing that Australia's visitor market was concentrated among a few major source countries.

Visitor Arrivals by Australian State

New South Wales recorded the highest number of international visitor arrivals in July 2026, followed by Queensland and Victoria. Western Australia attracted fewer visitors than these three leading states, while Tasmania, the Northern Territory and the ACT recorded the lowest arrival numbers. This indicates that visitor arrivals were concentrated in Australia's major states.

Main Reasons for Visiting Australia

Holiday travel was the leading reason for visiting Australia in 2025 to 2026, accounting for 45.4% of visitors. Visiting friends and relatives ranked second at 32.1%. Together, these two purposes accounted for approximately 77.5% of visitors, considerably exceeding the share represented by all other individual travel purposes.

International Visitor Arrivals by Source Country

Visitor arrivals varied across source countries and financial years, reflecting changes in Australia's international tourism markets. The pandemic period disrupted arrivals across these markets, followed by a recovery in subsequent years. Differences between countries indicate that the recovery was not necessarily uniform across all source markets.

International Visitor Arrivals by Age and Sex

Visitor arrivals are distributed unevenly across age groups, with some age ranges contributing more visitors than others. Differences between the male and female distributions reveal whether particular age groups have greater representation from one sex. This shows that international visitors are not evenly distributed across demographic groups.

Monthly International Visitor Arrivals

International visitor arrivals fluctuated between January and July 2026, showing that visitor numbers varied throughout the period. The differences between monthly totals indicate changes in travel demand rather than a constant level of arrivals. These monthly variations may reflect seasonal travel patterns, although a longer time series would be needed to establish recurring seasonality.

Changes in International Visitor Spending

International visitor spending experienced substantial disruption during the COVID-19 period as travel restrictions affected inbound tourism. Spending subsequently recovered as international travel resumed, with increases in the later years indicating a recovery from the pandemic-related decline. The changes over time highlight the vulnerability of tourism spending to major disruptions in international travel.

Changes in Australian State Rankings for Visitor Arrivals

The ranking trends show differences in the relative positions of Australian states and territories between 2020 and 2025. States that remain near the top maintain stronger positions in visitor arrivals, while changes in line positions indicate shifts in relative rankings. These movements show whether the distribution of visitor arrivals across states remained stable or changed over the period.

International Airline Connectivity to Australia

Passenger traffic is distributed unevenly across the airline-country connections represented in the dataset. Thicker links identify airline-country pairs with higher recorded passenger traffic, while thinner links represent lower volumes. The network highlights the relative importance of different connections within the selected records, rather than representing all international airline connectivity to Australia.

Tourism Expenditure by Product Category

Restaurant and takeaway meals recorded the highest tourism expenditure in 2024 to 2025, followed by long-distance passenger transport and vehicle repair, maintenance and fuel. Taxi and rideshare services recorded substantially lower expenditure. This shows that expenditure was concentrated in several major product categories rather than being distributed evenly across all tourism-related products and services.
